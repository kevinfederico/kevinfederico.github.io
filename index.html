My name is Kevin Federico. I am a PhD student in theoretical phyiscs at the University of Colorado, Boulder, advised by Chris Akers and Andy Lucas. I have broad interests.

Currently, I am thinking about:

  • What role does computational complexity play in nature?
    • Is it possible for a computationally limited observer to distinguish an effective description from a fundamental theory?
    • Can (quantum) computational simplicity be used as an organizing principle for the laws of physics (quantum field theory) in the same ways that symmetries can?
  • How can we learn about the physics of exotic systems by studying the physics of simple systems, and vice versa?
    • What makes a Hamiltonian holographic?
    • What would experimental diagnostics of an emergent spacetime look like?
  • What is the point of science?
    • How do we build a system for the future which rewards the right values?
    • Why do mathematicians never come to blows? Why do philosophers?

To the end of the above questions I work mainly with toy models of black holes and quantum field theory. I am continually excited by discussion with my peers and I like to learn as much as I possibly can. In the past I have worked on:

  • The absence of localized intersecting sources in supergravity
  • Black hole explosions as laboratories for new physics?
Outside of physics I find a life sustained by climbing, music, poetry, philosophy, fermentation, etc etc, etc. I am a generalist to first order and--like Wittgenstein--am by nature a hedgehog but slowly becoming a fox.
